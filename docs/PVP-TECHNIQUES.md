# Sword PvP techniques and their counters

A cleaned-up, complete version of an advanced sword PvP tutorial (YouTube `NinYq0LcqS8`, "everything I learned
training against top sword pvpers", plus a bonus knockback chapter by a guest, Theobald; for Hoplite PvP, MCC Island
and 1.9+ sword fighting in general). The raw transcript is `tactiq-free-transcript-NinYq0LcqS8.txt` in the project
folder; this file says the same thing in order, in plain steps, with nothing left out. Every technique and every
counter has an ID (T1, C1, ...) so code and notes can point at it.

Parts 1 to 6 are the tutorial. Part 7 (the counter table) and part 8 (notes for SwordAI) are ours, built from it.

---

## Part 0. Words used, and the basics the tutorial assumes

The tutorial assumes these are already known ("not the simple stuff like how to W-tap or how to punish crit"):

- **Sprint hit (KB hit):** a hit made while sprinting. It deals extra knockback and ends your sprint.
- **W-tap:** let go of W (or re-press it) between hits to restart the sprint, so the next hit is a sprint hit again.
- **Crit:** a hit made while falling (not sprinting, not on the ground). It does 1.5x damage but little knockback.
- **Punish crit (B1):** the crit you get from falling *after someone attacks you*. Their hit knocks you up; you crit
  them on the way down. Strong, but it leaves you open (see T1).
- **Trade:** an exchange of hits between two players. "Winning the trade" = coming out ahead on damage.
- **Combo:** a chain of stuns (see T3): you keep hitting them while they can't hit back.
- **Fly out of the trade:** let the knockback carry you away (usually by holding S) so they can't follow up.
- **Hit selecting:** waiting for the opponent's hit, then hitting right after it. The deflection (T4) is the same idea.
- **S-tap:** a short press of S to control spacing (stay just out of their reach while staying in yours).

## Part 1. Double crits

### T1. Double crit (beats punish crits)
- **What:** after your first crit, immediately jump and crit the other player again.
- **Why it works:** a player who punish-crits you (B1) is falling onto you, and that leaves them open. Your second crit
  lands on them, so you win the crit trade with an extra crit.
- **Only works if the opponent tries to punish-crit you.**
- **Countered by:** a sprint hit (C1). If they sprint-hit you instead of punish-critting, you get sent flying. That can
  put you in a really bad spot unless you fly out of the trade (C2).

#### How to know when to double crit (tips)
- **T1a. Bait the punish crit:** jump in *close* to the opponent with your first crit. This often provokes them into
  punish-critting you, which sets up an easy double crit.
- **T1b. Throw their aim off:** go around them or behind them as you double crit. If their aim is thrown off, the
  double crit is close to guaranteed.
- **T1c. Start with a punish crit:** the same idea from a punish crit. After you punish-crit them, immediately jump
  and crit again. This out-damages players who don't double crit you back in crit trades.

### C1. Counter to a double crit: sprint-hit instead of punish-critting
- When you expect a double crit, don't go for the punish crit. Sprint-hit them, which knocks them away.

### C2. Escape when your double crit gets sprint-hit
- You go for a double crit and they sprint-hit you instead of punish-critting: **immediately hold S**.
- Done right, you fly safely out of the trade and you are still up on damage.

### T2. Standing crit (a double crit started with a sprint hit)
- **What:** start with a sprint hit. As soon as the hit lands, jump straight up and crit them on your way down.
- **Why:** it punishes an opponent who tries to punish-crit you, exactly like a double crit.
- **When:** you sprint-hit them but they did not take enough knockback to be put in a combo. Instead of letting them
  punish-crit you again and again, the standing crit counters them.
- Also the main counter to jump resetting (C7).

## Part 2. Stuns: starting them and countering them

### T3. Stun
- **What:** a sprint hit that knocks the opponent far enough away that they *can't hit you back*.
- Combos are a chain of stuns.
- A stun is not only for combos. It sets up free damage of any kind: crits, double hits, or both.
- Being able to start stuns and counter them is essential for winning trades consistently.

### Ways to start a stun

#### T4. Deflection
- **What:** sprint-hit the opponent *immediately after they attack you* (the same thing as hit selecting).
- A well-timed, well-spaced deflection stuns them.
- **Timing and spacing matter a lot.** Too late, or too close, and they hit you *through* your deflection, and there
  is no stun.
- **Ping changes the right timing and spacing.** Against higher-ping players it may be easier to stun them with
  reverse deflections (T5).
- **If you keep getting hit through your deflections,** adjust your spacing and timing a little at a time until you
  stun them consistently.

#### T5. Reverse deflection
- **What:** sprint-hit your opponent *immediately after they use a deflection on you*: you deflect their deflection.
- **Only works if their deflection did not stun you.** So it doesn't work in every situation.
- Good against higher-ping players (T4).

#### T6. Make them miss, then stun
- When the opponent goes to attack you, stop moving or hold S, so their swing misses.
- **As soon as you see them miss,** your next sprint hit stuns them, provided you aren't too close.
- **T6a. Strafing:** strafe side to side as you fight, so they miss.
- Every miss by the opponent is a chance for a free stun.

#### T7. Three-block hit (at the start of a trade)
- **What:** sprint-hit the opponent *before they are close enough to hit you back*.
- You need to be **running at full sprint toward them** when you hit.
- If they are *not* at full sprint toward you, they often can't hit you back, as long as you hit from far enough.

#### T8. Hit a jumper while they rise
- If the opponent jumps at you and you hit them *as they are rising* from their jump, that is usually a free stun.

#### Caution
- A skilled opponent can stun *you* with exactly the same methods. Be careful entering trades.

### Ways to counter a stun

#### C3. Jump and hold S (fly out)
- **What:** when the opponent stuns you, jump and hold S, so that you fly out of the trade.
- **Common mistake:** holding S only a hit or two *after* being stunned. This can work for getting out of combos, but
  you take more damage than you need to. Against experienced players one stun is enough to put you in a trade that
  shreds your health.
- **Do it right:** hold S *right before* you get stunned. This means predicting when you are about to be stunned,
  which comes with experience: you learn to see when you are open to a stun before it happens.

#### C4. When holding S doesn't get you out, and its fix
- **The counter to C3:** if they know you will hold S when hit, and they are close enough, they sprint-jump toward
  you as they attack, which keeps you in their range.
- **The fix:** attack them *right before* you hold S. That usually kills their momentum if they try to sprint-jump
  after you.
- Another reason to hold S *before* the stun: once you are already in a combo, nothing stops them from sprint-jumping
  after you.

#### C5. Jump resetting
- See Part 3. It counters stuns and does much more.

## Part 3. Jump resetting

### T9 / C5. Jump reset
- **What:** a way to take *reduced horizontal knockback*: press jump at the same moment you take damage.
- The timing is tricky. Practise it with a friend or a bot until you are sure of it.
- **Uses:** it counters stuns, starts stuns, and gets crits.
- **Risk:** mistime the jump and you get sent flying when they hit you. You need a good sense of *when the opponent is
  going to hit you*.

#### The best and safest moments to jump reset
- **T9a. Against combos.** Most players combo to a set rhythm, so they are not hard to jump-reset out of. They usually
  hit you the moment you land on the ground with every hit, and that is when to jump reset.
- **T9b. Against deflections.** Many players abuse deflections (T4), which gives you a free jump reset. If you know
  they will deflect, press jump the moment *after you attack them*, so you jump-reset their deflection. You can do
  this as often as you like. As long as your jump matches each of their sprint hits, they can hardly hit you away.
- **T9c. Against three-block hits (T7).** You can tell a player is going for a three-block hit when they aren't
  jumping around, just sprinting straight at you. Sprint at them, hit them as soon as they are in range, and press
  jump at the same time. You jump-reset their hit, which gives you a free crit as you fall.

### Counters to jump resetting

#### C6. Standing crit against a jump reset
- Sprint-hit them, jump straight up, and crit on your way down (T2). If they jump-reset your sprint hit, they jump
  straight into your crit.
- **Only works if your sprint hit stuns them.** If it doesn't, they sprint-hit you away before your crit lands.
- So it works best right after a well-timed deflection (T4), or at the end of a combo.

#### C7. Delayed sprint hit
- When they try to jump-reset, *delay* your sprint hit. They jump before you hit them, so they are in the air when
  your hit lands and they fly away.

#### Notes on both counters
- Both need you to *predict when they will jump reset*. That comes with experience.
- A jump reset gives *reduced* knockback, not zero. With good spacing you can still stun and combo a player who
  jump-resets. It is a lot harder, and S-tapping helps.

## Part 4. Jumping techniques (uppercut and sprint cut)

Knowing when and how to jump gives a massive advantage. Two jumping techniques are commonly used to stun players.

### T10. Uppercut
- **What:** jump, and sprint-hit the opponent *at the peak of your jump*.
- **Why:** it reaches someone who would otherwise be too far away. Your movement speed is highest at the start of a
  jump, which is enough to catch up if they are not too far.
- **Often combined with S right after the sprint hit.** That keeps some distance and keeps you out of their range.
- **Best time:** right after you stun them. For example, deflection (T4) into uppercut is an easy way to start combos
  against less experienced players.

### T11. Sprint cut
- Like the uppercut, but the sprint hit is timed *as you fall* from your jump, not at the peak. Two kinds:

#### T11a. Lunge
- **What:** sprint-jump forward and sprint-hit them as you fall.
- You move forward much faster than when sprinting without jumping.
- **Use:** extra hits on players who hold S out of a trade (C3).

#### T11b. Hops
- **What:** jumps where you barely move forward each time. You stay *outside their reach* while holding them in a
  combo.
- Much harder than lunges.
- **How:**
  1. While sprinting forward, let go of W and jump right after. Timed right, this kills all your forward momentum
     and you jump straight up.
  2. After the jump, hold W to start sprinting again. You won't move forward much while in the air.
  3. When you are about to land, release W and press space to jump again. **Release W before you jump**, or you just
     sprint-jump forward.
  4. Repeat for every jump.
  5. Time your sprint hit *as you fall* from each jump, *right before you release W*. Otherwise it is not a sprint
     hit.
- With practice, hops hold players in combos as long as they don't back out.
- **Why hops are strong:**
  1. **They disguise your sprint hits as crits.** Most good players don't hold S when they think someone is going to
     crit them, because a crit doesn't deal enough knockback to knock you out of the trade. As long as they are
     expecting a crit, hops keep them in the combo.
  2. **They build momentum for a lunge.** (Parkour example: a jump from standing goes less far than one made out of a
     hop.) If you hop and they hold S right after, the extra momentum lets you lunge and land extra hits.
     - Example from the tutorial: hold W right after a hop and sprint-jump forward. You can crit while holding W,
       because you are not resetting your sprint, if they hold S right after your hop. This lets you chase them down
       with extra crits.
- **T11c. Alternate hops and lunges** whenever they hold S. If you can predict their S, you can keep players in combos
  that are extremely hard to get out of. This is one of the hardest sword techniques: a lot of practice and
  prediction.

## Part 5. Knockback mechanics (bonus chapter, Theobald)

### K1. Mid-air hits push them the way they are already going
- Hitting someone in mid-air knocks them in the direction they are *already moving*.
- So if you hit someone who is critting you (falling toward you), they take little knockback and stick to the floor.

### K2. The golden rule of knockback
- **Don't knockback-hit someone who is falling,** whether during a combo or while they are critting you.
- **Wait until they are on the ground or jumping up** to get the extra knockback. (Matches T8.)

### K3. The flip side: make them hit you while you fall
- Space yourself so that when you jump, the opponent is *forced to hit you as you are falling*.
- This reduces your own knockback. Use it to crit again and again, or even to start a combo.

## Part 7. Who beats whom (built from the tutorial)

| If they do | Answer with | That answer fails when |
|---|---|---|
| Punish crit (B1) after your hit | Double crit (T1) / standing crit (T2) | They sprint-hit instead of punish-critting (C1) |
| Double crit (T1) | Sprint hit, not a punish crit (C1) | They hold S at once and fly out still up on damage (C2) |
| Sprint hit (stun attempt) | Jump reset (T9), or jump + S *before* it (C3) | They standing-crit (C6) or delay the hit (C7); they sprint-jump after your S (C4) |
| Deflection (T4) | Reverse deflection (T5); jump reset right after your attack (T9b) | Their deflection already stunned you (T5); the jump is mistimed (T9) |
| Three-block hit (T7) | Hit as they enter reach + jump reset (T9c): free crit | They delay the hit (C7), or the jump is mistimed |
| Jump in at you (rising) | Hit them while they rise (T8, K2) | The hit comes late, when they are already falling (K1); on a server the knockback itself comes late (ours, part 8) |
| Jump in at you (falling, critting) | Don't KB-hit them (K2); let it land, punish crit (B1) | They double crit (T1) |
| Jump reset (T9) | Standing crit (C6), delayed sprint hit (C7), good spacing + S-tap | C6: your sprint hit did not stun, so they sprint-hit you away first; C7: they did not jump |
| Holding S out (C3) | Lunge (T11a), sprint-jump chase (C4), hops into lunges (T11c) | They attacked right before the S, killing your momentum (C4) |
| Swinging at you | Make them miss: stop / S / strafe, then stun (T6) | You are too close when you hit: no stun |
| Hops (T11b) holding a combo | Back out (hops hold a combo only while you stay in), C3 before the hit | They lunge after you (T11a, T11c) |

The pattern: **every technique wins only if you predicted what they would do**. Punish crit beats a plain hit, double
crit beats the punish crit, sprint hit beats the double crit, jump reset beats the sprint hit, delayed sprint hit beats
the jump reset. A player wins by reading habits (rhythm, what they did after the last hit, how they come in) and picking
the answer one step ahead.

## Part 8. Notes for SwordAI (ours, not the tutorial's)

What each technique looks like in ticks and keys, for the opponents and for the agent's training. Mechanics we have
checked in this code base:
- A hit lifts the target only if it is **on the ground** (vertical speed set to min(0.4, vy/2 + 0.4)). This is why a
  falling jumper takes little knockback (K1, K2), and why a ground hit lifts you into a punish crit (B1).
- A crit needs falling, not on the ground, and **not sprinting**. That is why the hop's sprint hit has to come before W
  is released (T11b), and why sprint hits and crits are different hits.
- On a server a hit's knockback reaches the player a few ticks late (ping). A jumper you hit can still be in reach for
  its crit (the 23 October "meet then crit back" problem).
- The sword is back again after 12.5 ticks (1.6 attacks a second). A jump lasts about 12 ticks: up for 6, down for 6.

Cues the agent can read (it needs memory of the last few seconds to predict, as the user put it: a human knows what
the other will do):
- **Coming to three-block hit you (T7, T9c):** sprinting straight at it, not jumping.
- **Going to punish-crit:** it was lifted by your hit and its sword is ready.
- **Jump resetter (T9):** it jumped on the tick of your last hits. Count it per fight.
- **Holds S after a hit (C3):** it moves straight away after hits. Count it per fight.
- **Combo rhythm (T9a):** the ticks between its hits are regular, and it hits when you land.
- **Deflector (T4):** it sprint-hits right after your swings.
- **Hopper (T11b):** small jumps in place in reach, with sprint hits on the way down.

Opponent design rule from the user (23 Oct): no one-trick, exaggerated bots. Each opponent draws a style: how likely it
is to use each technique above, its timing skill, its ping. It reacts to what the agent does (it switches to C7 if the
agent jump-resets a lot, to C1 if the agent punish-crits), so the agent has to *learn* the techniques and the counters
rather than one exploit.
